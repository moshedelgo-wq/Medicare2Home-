Frequently Asked Questions & Information Center

Initial answers regarding medication import, non-covered medications, delivery, medical tourism and service for professional organizations.

Medication Import

Can a medication that is not registered in Israel be imported?

Yes. A medication that is not registered in Israel can be imported, subject to the required approvals. In relevant cases, documents such as Form 29G and a physician’s prescription are required. Medicare reviews the request and documentation and, when necessary, works to source the medication through suppliers abroad and import it to Israel in accordance with the applicable regulatory requirements.

Which approvals and documents are required?

In most cases involving a request for a medication that is not registered in Israel, Form 29G and a physician’s prescription are required. Requirements may vary depending on the medication and the relevant pathway, so the Medicare team reviews each request and advises the patient if additional documents are needed.

How long does it take to receive a medication imported from abroad?

Delivery time varies according to the medication, supplier availability and the import process, but in most cases it ranges from two to four weeks. Once the request and required documents are received, the Medicare team checks availability and supply options and provides an update accordingly.

Is a prescription required?

Yes. To order a prescription medication in Israel, a prescription from a physician in Israel is required. When the medication is not registered in Israel, or when its use requires approval under Regulation 29, the relevant Form 29 is also required, depending on the case. The Medicare team reviews the documents received and advises the patient if anything else is needed before the order can proceed.

Special & Non-Covered Medications

What if the medication is not included in the Israeli health basket?

Even when a medication is not included in the Israeli health basket, it may still be possible to obtain it through Medicare. For a patient with private health insurance, if the medication is covered under the policy and the insurer approves the request, the insurance company provides a financial authorization directly to Medicare, and Medicare handles the medication order and delivery to the patient. A patient without private insurance can order the medication privately and arrange payment directly with Medicare. If the medication is unavailable in Israel, Medicare can also assess the option of sourcing it abroad and importing it to Israel, subject to the required approvals.

Delivery

Does Medicare provide home delivery?

Yes. Medicare delivers medications anywhere in Israel, directly to the address coordinated with the patient. Delivery is carried out according to the transport conditions required for the medication, including medications that require controlled conditions such as refrigeration.

Does Medicare deliver medications that require refrigeration?

Yes. Medicare also supplies medications that require refrigeration and controlled transport conditions. Medications are transported according to their required storage and transport conditions, with the appropriate conditions maintained throughout the delivery process.

Medical Tourism

Can Medicare assist a patient coming to Israel from abroad for medical treatment?

Absolutely. Medicare serves patients coming to Israel for medical treatment and those assisting them, and can coordinate the patient’s medication needs throughout the entire stay in Israel. Medicare can be contacted before the patient arrives, so prescriptions and the list of required medications can be reviewed and availability checked in advance. If a medication is unavailable in Israel, Medicare can source it through its international supplier network and pursue import to Israel, subject to the required approvals. During the patient’s stay, delivery can be coordinated to a hospital, medical center, hotel or medical-tourism agency. The service does not end at hospital discharge: Medicare can continue supporting the patient until the end of the stay in Israel and, when needed, can supply medication for continued treatment after the patient returns home, subject to the prescription and applicable requirements. The patient, a family member or a medical-tourism agent can also contact Medicare’s customer service team and pharmacists for service and pharmacist guidance in Hebrew, English and Russian.

Service & Pharmacist Consultation

Can I receive pharmacist consultation and support?

Yes. Medicare’s pharmacists provide professional pharmacist consultation and support to anyone who requests assistance, including patients, family members, medical-tourism agents, insurance companies and professional partners. The consultation is provided within the pharmacist’s professional scope and does not replace medical advice or a physician’s clinical decision.

In which languages is service available?

Medicare provides customer service and pharmacist consultation in Hebrew, English and Russian.

Insurance Companies

Does Medicare work directly with insurance companies?

Yes. Medicare works directly with insurance companies. When an insurer approves coverage for a medication, it provides a financial authorization directly to Medicare for the order, subject to the policy terms and the insurer’s approval.

The information on this website is general and does not replace medical or pharmacist advice. Medication supply and services are subject to applicable law, prescriptions and required approvals.
